Law Between Calculation and Judgment

The Future of Law: Artificial Intelligence Meets Human Judgment

What does Artificial Intelligence mean for the legal practice of tomorrow? Christoph Holz — digital ethicist, graduate computer scientist (Dipl.-Inform.) and court-sworn IT expert witness — explains why algorithms are by no means racist, why hallucination is a blessing, and why neural language models are a dead end. And he shows how deontic AI finally resolves Jørgensen's Dilemma. Using vivid examples, Holz demonstrates what AI can do in law, where its limits lie and where the journey is heading — entertaining, interactive and deliberately optimistic. Those who understand how, win.

Five Thoughts from the Talk

Anyone who believes that neural AI sometimes hallucinates is wrong. It always hallucinates. But with a little luck, it sometimes happens to agree with reality.

For some, it is an imposition. But responsibility is what makes us human. And responsibility can never be digitized.

If you hoped AI would finally rid us of lawyers, I have to disappoint you. When one lawyer with AI delivers the same as five without, there will be five times more cases. More people can finally afford legal certainty. If that isn't progress.

Generative AI — neural networks in general — is based on statistics. And statistics is and remains the dictatorship of the average.

Ethics are uncomfortable. Otherwise they would be compliance.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance together with the organiser — here, for instance, to AI-drafted briefs, legal tech and liability questions.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — and courage at that: vivid stories like the judge algorithm from California provide talking points that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the law firm.

LegalTech Angel Investor

Christoph Holz knows LegalTech from contract automation to regulation. At Cake Equity, where he is an early-stage investor, corporate law becomes software: shares, options, SAFEs and ESOPs are created in a legally watertight way at the click of a button instead of via a law firm.
