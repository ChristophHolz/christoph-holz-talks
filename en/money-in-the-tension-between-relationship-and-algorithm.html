Money in the tension between relationship and algorithm

Digital payments and the future of the financial system in the age of AI

Money has always been nothing but information we use to communicate value to each other. The Sumerians invented bank money 5,000 years ago — two and a half millennia before the first coin. Are the average 117 euros in cash in every German wallet really our last anonymous island? What happens in 2029, when the digital euro arrives, programmable right into your living room? When AI agents negotiate autonomously and smart contracts replace bank, lawyer and government — could money itself become obsolete in data-rich markets? Six thought experiments — and one single question: What is a relationship worth when the algorithm does everything faster, cheaper and more tamper-proof?

Three Thoughts from the Talk

The freedom of cash ends at 117 euros. That's the average amount people carry. Sadly, the dirty deeds I'd like to keep secret cost more.

In data-rich markets, money may vanish. What remains is the oldest currency: trust, which no agent can negotiate for you.

The Sumerians recorded value long before anyone minted a coin. Money was never the metal, but the promise — and promises live on relationship.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to the digital euro, AI agents, and smart contracts.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the last clay tablet that forgets spark conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the cooperative bank.
