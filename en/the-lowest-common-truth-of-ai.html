The lowest common truth of AI

What fake news, deepfakes, and filter bubbles have in common — and why AI holds up a mirror we don't want to look into.

Our brain doesn't crave truth — it craves dopamine. That's why fake news, deepfakes, and conspiracies sell better than any fact-check. GPT4 stays politically correct and silent. DeepSeek knows nothing of a Tiananmen Massacre. Grok finds Elon more famous than Jesus. What's left is the smallest common truth. And if it offends no one, is it still truth — or just banality? And if the AI says it, it must be true. The truth can be spoken, whether it's true or not. A keynote on why, of all things, the hallucinating algorithms force us into critical thinking.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Grok claims a certain Elon is more famous than Jesus. China's DeepSeek knows of no massacre at Tiananmen Square, and GPT struggles with abortion. This is the regulating-away of truth.

Is it still truth if it hurts no one—or merely banality.

In the past, people searched for truths on Google; today, many use ChatGPT and the like. While search engines merely link, LLMs formulate their own answers. Whoever leaves out controversial topics can avoid questions of liability.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to your industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to disinformation, deepfakes and critical thinking.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like AI's smallest common truth spark conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens your judgment.
