The Future Is Well Insured

The Insurance Industry Between Artificial Intelligence and Human Trust.

Insurers are the oldest statisticians in the world — artificial intelligence is their home game. Christoph Holz — digital ethicist, graduate computer scientist (Dipl.-Inform.) and TV expert — bridges the gap from classical actuarial mathematics to the learning machine: what AI can really do in underwriting, claims and service, where its limits lie, and why the industry's proverbial caution is its actual product. Using vivid examples from Silicon Valley, the boardroom and the claims file, he shows how the statistics of the average become a future that does justice to the individual — entertaining, interactive and deliberately optimistic. Those who understand how, win.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

A statistic is at best generally right and in the individual case usually wrong. There's nothing more to say about LLMs.

The problem isn't that these AI systems get too good. The problem is that we trust them too much.

Generative AI — neural networks in general — is based on statistics. And statistics is and remains the dictatorship of the average.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, on regulation, the skills shortage and AI in the claims process.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — namely courage: vivid stories like the 9-million-dollar man spark conversations well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the industry.

Christoph Holz's Expertise

Court-sworn IT expert witness

As a court-sworn IT expert witness, Holz doesn't offer opinions but expert reports — with an authority recognized by Austria's courts.

LegalTech Angel Investor

Christoph Holz knows LegalTech from contract automation to regulation. At Cake Equity, where he is an early-stage investor, corporate law becomes software: shares, options, SAFEs and ESOPs are created in a legally watertight way at the click of a button instead of via a law firm. And at Free Street, formerly Braveno, he is on board as a highly regulated crypto exchange turns compliance and licensing into the actual product. On stage, Holz answers the question of why today's most exciting startups emerge at the border between code and law, and how contracts become software — and vice versa.

FinTech

As a business angel, Holz roams across the FinTech world. He's an investor in Free Street, formerly Braveno, a highly regulated crypto exchange, in the equity platform Cake Equity, and in the crypto card TenX. On a future-focused trip to Lagos he witnessed Africa's FinTech boom firsthand — from the digital bank Kuda to the payment service Paga to Flutterwave. And at GenTwo in Zurich, he saw how financial engineering can securitize almost any asset. On stage, Holz answers the question of why money has long been software and where the next billions will emerge.

Studied the Code of Hammurabi at the Louvre – the oldest code

For Holz, the Code of Hammurabi in the Louvre is society's first piece of programming — laws as code, carved in stone. And with its caravan rules, it's also the oldest documented insurance in the world.
