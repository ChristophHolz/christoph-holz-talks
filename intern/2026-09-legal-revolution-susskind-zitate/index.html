Richard Susskind — Zitatsammlung

Wortgenaue Zitate des Gastes aus dem Podcast Legal AI Lab, Folge „This Will Change Every Lawyer's Career“

Sprecher   Prof. Richard Susskind
Sendung   Legal AI Lab, Host Hielke Bruinsma, 25.09.2025
Quelle    https://youtu.be/Amad6yV-yR0
Zweck     Aphorismen-Sammlung / Keynote Legal Revolution, Berlin, 22.09.2026
Umfang    42 Zitate, alle wörtlich, nur Susskind

I · Kurzfrist- und Langfrist-KI

1
„Long-term AI in law is not about equipping lawyers. It's about empowering non-lawyers to conduct legal work for themselves.“
Sein selbst markierter Kernsatz — the most vital point I'll probably make today. Stärkster Einzelsatz der ganzen Folge.
2
„We are essentially equipping many of our young lawyers for the 20th century rather than for the 21st century.“
Zur Juristenausbildung. Kurz, hart, bühnentauglich.
3
„A lot of the legal technology community in their enthusiasm confuse what's technologically possible today with what's likely to happen in the near future.“
Seine Unterscheidung advance of technology vs. adoption of technology. Gute Bremse gegen Hype.
4
„Self-evidently beneficial technologies are not always immediately taken up by the profession.“
Aus 40 Jahren Beobachtung. Als Beleg für langsame Adoption trotz klaren Nutzens.
5
„While I do talk very broadly about pervasive, transformative, irreversible change, I'm also realistic in thinking that both in law firms and in our court systems, these changes are not going to happen overnight.“
Seine eigene Relativierung. Nützlich, wenn man ihm nicht Alarmismus unterstellen will.
6
„We have to distinguish between automation on the one hand, and innovation and elimination on the other hand.“
Die begriffliche Achse des ganzen Gesprächs.
7
„The idea of elimination is when AI is going to take away — not the jobs, although that may happen — but will take away the problems for which lawyers used to exist.“
Die eigentliche Pointe hinter „Elimination“. Deutlich stärker als jede Jobverlust-Prognose.
8
„I think there's a bonanza just now. Look at the sense of excitement, that oh my goodness, we've got a great new tool. And I'm saying to you, you're going to reach the end of the runway with that tool towards the end of this decade.“
Warnung an die Legal-Tech-Euphorie. Das Bild von der zu Ende gehenden Startbahn ist sehr zitierfähig.

II · Warum es Professionen gibt

9
„The professions don't exist in nature. We've created them. They're a construct.“
Der schärfste Satz des Abschnitts. Entzieht dem Berufsstand jede Naturrechts-Aura.
10
„The law is not there to provide a livelihood for lawyers any more than ill health is there to provide a living for doctors.“
Der Aphorismus schlechthin. Funktioniert isoliert, ohne jeden Kontext.
11
„We train people, we pay them handsomely — I call it the grand bargain — in return for the prestige, the payment and so forth, we have these trusted advisers who keep their knowledge up to date.“
Seine Definition des grand bargain. Begriff aus The Future of the Professions, 2015.
12
„The professions exist to solve a fairly clear category of problem in society. And the problem is one of knowledge.“
Reduktion der Profession auf ein Verteilungsproblem von Wissen — Voraussetzung für sein ganzes Argument.
13
„Until AI systems came along, there wasn't really an option to using other human beings.“
Warum die Nachfrage historisch so groß war — und warum das jetzt kippt.
14
„We as humans, as we've done throughout the history of humanity, will respond to opportunities and adapt as necessary.“
Sein optimistischer Konter auf die Jobverlust-Angst.
15
„When there's a breakthrough in medicine, the question that people don't ask is what does this mean for doctors. They think, what does it mean for patients?“
Rhetorisch die stärkste Zurechtweisung der Branche. Sehr gut als Zwischenruf verwendbar.

III · Systeme bauen statt konkurrieren

16
„The mindset we need is to be building the systems that replace our old ways of working.“
Seine zentrale Handlungsempfehlung, in einem Satz.
17
„Do you want to compete with these emerging systems, or do you want to build them?“
Die Zuspitzung als Frage. Ideal als Publikumsfrage von der Bühne.
18
„If you believe these systems are becoming increasingly capable — and all the evidence suggests they are — you're painting yourself into a corner.“
Gegen die Strategie, sich auf das Nicht-Automatisierbare zurückzuziehen. Er nennt sie a dreadful strategy.
19
„There'll just be less and less that humans will be able to do that machines can't do.“
Direkte Gegenposition zu jedem „Das kann KI nie“. Gute Reibungsfläche für eigene Grenzen-Argumente.
20
„You are a content provider. Now that's not the way you've looked at your work in the past.“
Antwort auf „Ich bin kein Techniker“. Provokant gegenüber dem Selbstbild der Kanzlei.
21
„You move away from being a consultative advisor to being an organization that designs, develops, implements, sells and maintains systems.“
Der Rollenwechsel der Kanzlei, präzise formuliert.
22
„Who develops these systems? Who ensures they're up to date? Who tests these systems? Who gives the market confidence that these systems have been subject to appropriate assurance tests?“
Die Fragenkaskade, aus der er die neue Berufsrolle ableitet. Wirkt gesprochen sehr gut.
23
„I think it would be quite hard in the future in any job not to be to some extent technical.“
Knapp, allgemein verwendbar, nicht auf Juristen beschränkt.
24
„If you believe that in the long run AI systems will themselves develop the next generation of systems, then over time even this new role for the legal professional will fade. But that'll be, I suspect, in the late '30s, early '40s.“
Selbstrelativierung mit Datum. Wichtig: seine Empfehlung hat ein Ablaufdatum, das er selbst nennt.

IV · Prävention statt Reparatur

25
„If we can put the fence at the top of the cliff rather than the ambulance at the bottom, we're not problem solving, we're problem avoiding.“
Das stärkste Bild der Folge. Steht für sich, braucht keinen Kontext.
26
„This is what general counsel say to me all the time: we don't want a big problem well resolved, we want to avoid the pitfalls.“
Als Beleg aus der Praxis. Der Kontrast well resolved vs. avoided trägt den Satz.
27
„You often need legal insight to know if or when best to consult a lawyer.“
Sein paradox of traditional reactive service, erstmals 1996 formuliert. Bündige Fassung.
28
„I reckon we'll have AI systems that will be able to roam around the information systems of major organizations and find early markers of legal problems.“
Sein konkretestes Zukunftsbild — Agenten als juristischer Bluttest.
29
„To some extent AI will, if not resolve, it will dissolve the paradox of traditional reactive legal service.“
Das Wortspiel resolve / dissolve ist im Deutschen schwer zu halten — im Original zitieren.
30
„One of the great skills we have as professionals, of course, is categorizing problems. That's what we're really good at.“
Was er der Profession als Kernkompetenz zugesteht — und was Systeme künftig übernehmen sollen.

V · Access to Justice

31
„The world over, our legal system is creaking. For most people, legal services and dispute resolution, for example, are unaffordable.“
Seine Diagnose in einem Satz. creaking ist das Wort, das hängen bleibt.
32
„Our current offering is unaffordable. We have to recognize that it's inaccessible. We have to understand that it's unintelligible for many people.“
Dreiklang gegen den Halluzinations-Einwand. Er zählt sich ausdrücklich selbst zur Branche.
33
„The market will show no loyalty at all to conventional lawyers if they're finding their problems solved in a way that's cheap and cheerful, quick, convenient.“
Die härteste Marktprognose der Folge.
34
„If you have no other way of addressing your legal issue, I don't think there's a nervousness there. I think for most people, well, we might as well have a go.“
Seine Relativierung von Halluzinationen im Access-to-Justice-Kontext. Achtung: stärkster Reibungspunkt zur eigenen Position.
35
„That's the great excitement of AI: that it is empowering people to do work for themselves that historically would have required deep expertise and deep pockets as well.“
Sein positives Gegenbild. deep expertise and deep pockets als Paar.

VI · AGI, Aliens, Regulierung

36
„Everyone goes in for what I call not-us thinking — that AI applies to everyone except us, because we're different.“
Prägnanter Begriff für die verbreitetste Abwehrhaltung. Sofort einsetzbar.
37
„What I'm trying to discourage in the legal world is what I rather critically call surface thinking about AI.“
Sein zweiter Kampfbegriff, neben not-us thinking.
38
„In 2022, if you'd asked most people in the AI development world when will we have AGI, most of them said 20 to 40 years, if ever. If you ask that same question today, most are saying 5 to 10 years.“
Die einzige belastbare Zahlenangabe der Folge. Als Datenpunkt zitierbar.
39
„Now is the time to think: what do we feel are the moral boundaries of AI systems that can perform at this level?“
Seine ethische Kernforderung — Regulierung vorausdenken statt hinterherlaufen.
40
„We can't see inside one another's minds as human beings — what philosophers call the problem of other minds — but we assume there's something intelligent going on.“
Philosophischer Einschub. Anschlussfähig an eigene Argumente über Fühlen und Simulation.

VII · Vier Szenarien und Menschlichkeit

41
„The point about joint venture is we keep a little bit of a distance, and we retain our distinctive humanity.“
Seine gewählte Option von vieren — Takeover, Merger, Joint Venture, Termination.
42
„Saving humanity with and from AI is the defining challenge for our generation.“
Sein Schlusssatz und laut eigener Aussage sein running theme. Die Doppelpräposition with and from ist der ganze Witz — im Deutschen nur schwer zu retten.
