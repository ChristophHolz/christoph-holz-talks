First guest — Gregor Weihs

My first guest went to work in 1998 with four hundred metres of optical fibre.
He strung it across a university campus in Innsbruck.

Questions — statement first, then the question

  1. I hear you are becoming the new Rector in Innsbruck. Gregor, is there any job at that university that is harder?
  2. In the nineties this field had no funding programmes, no startups, no investors in the room. What made it worth going into?
  3. Stanford, Tokyo, Waterloo. Boston, Stuttgart, Zurich. Your CVs read like flight schedules. Which station changed you most?
  4. One of you tested Bell in 1998. The other squeezed light in 2015. Explain them so the investors here can retell it at the reception.
  5. Back then the whole field fitted into one conference room. When did you first come across each other's work?
  6. Everyone here wants to know what comes next. What are your groups working on that nobody in this room has on their radar?
  7. One of you runs the Cavendish. The other takes over a university. What is harder to control — a quantum system or a faculty?
  8. Spinning out used to look like a career risk. Today the first PhD student asks about the cap table. Gain or loss?
  9. Investors call you. Ministries call you. The press calls you. What do they always ask, and what should they be asking?
  10. There is more public money in quantum than the field has ever seen. Where does it land well, and where does it evaporate?
  11. Let us fast forward to 2036. What does quantum physics at a leading university look like?
  12. Every field has its founding voices. One line from a pioneer that has stayed with you?

Detail to fall back on

The 1998 Bell test. Alice and Bob, 400 metres apart across the Innsbruck campus. The measurement setting came from a quantum random generator — an LED on a beam splitter, two photomultipliers behind it. Every measurement finished inside 1.3 microseconds, the light travel time between stations. Data taken 1997 to 1999. Physical Review Letters, 7 December 1998.
The 2022 Nobel Prize. Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger, for exactly this class of experiment.
Squeezed light, 2015. Nature 525, 222–225. An artificial atom with a large optical dipole. A hundredfold higher photon rate than a natural atom. Quadrature variance 3 % below the vacuum limit, photon statistics still antibunched.
The Cavendish. Founded 1874. More than thirty Nobel Prizes. Thomson, Rutherford, Watson and Crick. Atatüre is a Bilkent graduate from Ankara.
Innsbruck. 130,000 inhabitants. AQT, Parity, the Zeilinger school. Useful for the question on national money.
