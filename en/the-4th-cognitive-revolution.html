The 4th Cognitive Revolution

How programming society gives us new capabilities

Every cognitive revolution costs an old skill and grants a new one: writing took our memory and gave us the archive. Now comes the fourth. We make algorithms — and then the algorithms make us: software is the codified worldview of its creators, and our society is being reprogrammed right now. Yet the same mental transformation that changes us can also give us new abilities — parallel attention instead of depth, broader instead of deeper. In thought experiments, Christoph Holz shows how the programming of society works — and how we can use it. Those who understand the how gain new abilities.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

First we shape our algorithms, and then our algorithms shape us.

Parallel attention — not distraction, but a different cognitive architecture. Broader rather than deeper.

Every time we gain a new mode of thinking, we give something up. Humans traded photographic thinking for language. What will we trade for AI?

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to speech, writing, and the printing press.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the silent update spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers society.
