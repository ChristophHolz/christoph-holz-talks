Magnifica Humanitas — The Pope and AI

Why, of all things, the world's oldest institution gives the smartest answer to artificial intelligence

The Catholic Church survived the plague, the printing press, Galileo, and Darwin — and spent 2000 years reflecting on the soul, dignity, and death. It's no coincidence that Pope Leo XIV grounded his name in the Digital Revolution: just as Leo XIII responded to the steam engine in 1891, the Church today responds to the thinking machine. When AI can imitate everything human, the oldest question returns: What is man? Christoph Holz — Rocket Scientist and digital ethicist — guides you with wit and depth through six thought experiments, from digital resurrection to dignity at the end of life. Magnifica humanitas: the magnificence of the human being that no machine can replace. Whoever grasps this, wins.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

It is not the institution with the fastest server that gives the best answer to AI, but the one with the longest experience in dignity.

What remains when AI takes over everything that can be optimized? The human being. With everything that cannot be optimized.

AI is not a technical term, but a philosophical creation myth. It represents computer science's eternal longing to create a life form of its own.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to human dignity, care, and resurrection.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the avatar of the late grandmother spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers people.
