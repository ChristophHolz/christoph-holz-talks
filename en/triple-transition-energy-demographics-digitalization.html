Triple Transition — Energy, Demographics, Digitalization

How Three Crises Become a Mandate to Shape the Future

Energy costs, the exit of the baby boomers and stalled digitalization hit every location at once. Most companies manage three separate crises — three budgets, three sets of worry lines. Christoph Holz flips the perspective: it's a single transformation, and all three share one answer. The gap demographics tears open is exactly the space automation grows into — no layoffs, no conflict. And the same intelligence that lifts scarce labor turns expensive energy from merely costly into controllable. Three operational levers, five thought experiments. Three worries become one mandate to shape the future. Those who understand how, win.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

When the boomer leaves, he takes forty years of silent knowledge with him. The most valuable thing in any company is in no manual — only AI can save it.

It's not about replacing people, but about filling positions for which there are no people left.

For decades we feared the robots would take our jobs. Demographics reverse the sentence: we are running out of people—and the machines are arriving just in time.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to the skills shortage, energy costs, and digitalization.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the empty position that will never be filled again spark conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the region.

Christoph Holz's Expertise

Energy

Christoph Holz has toured the future of energy where it's actually being made: in the control room of the Tokamak ASDEX Upgrade at the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, at Gauss Fusion (giga power plant) and Proxima Fusion (stellarator) — plus the SPRIND briefing on laser-driven fusion. Via a convertible loan to Emerald Horizon AG, he's putting his own money into thorium as the next generation of nuclear energy. Bringing him to the stage: Illwerke-VKW and the green transformation of Happy End by Rocket Science. On stage, he answers the question of how 200 grams of deuterium and 700 grams of lithium can supply a year's worth of electricity for 1,000 people.

Industrial robots

Ever since he built his first industrial articulated-arm robot in 1986, industrial robotics has never let go of Christoph Holz: in 2014 he saw the welding robots at Tesla in Fremont, and in 2026 their next generation at Magna Steyr on the G-Class assembly line. At ABB he explored cobots. On stage, he answers the question of why robots are leaving their cages — and why an industrial robot needs hands with five fingers when three would do.

Generative AI in Enterprise Use

On stage, Holz shows how companies turn ChatGPT, Anthropic & Co. into business success — and where the limits lie.
