The Power of the Misfits

The future philosophy of the most influential IT thinkers — Elon Musk, Peter Thiel, Bill Gates and co.

Mavericks shape our future more than any government — and barely anyone understands what they actually believe. Peter Thiel seeks the sovereign individual beyond institutions. Elon Musk wants to save humanity as a backup on Mars. Bill Gates wants to fix this world with data and innovation. Christoph Holz — himself a Silicon Valley insider — decodes the worldviews behind the tech titans: flee or fix, individual or species, pessimism or optimism. Because software is the codified worldview of its creators — and they program the world. Whoever understands how, wins.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Digitalization is the first revolution that isn't about seizing power, but about abolishing it.

The future is no longer shaped, it's coded. We nerds weren't prepared for that responsibility. We just wanted to play.

Digitalization is the emancipation movement of IT nerds. We turned our discrimination into world domination. That's how it's done.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to the philosophy of the future, worldviews, and tech titans.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — and that something is courage: vivid stories like Mars as a backup for the light of consciousness fuel conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the decision-makers.
