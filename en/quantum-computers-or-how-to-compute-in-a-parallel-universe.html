A Keynote by Christoph Holz Sets the Tone for Your Event

Quantum computers — or how to compute in a parallel universe

Hype and reality of the next generation of computing

Wouldn't it be handy if we could quickly crunch the numbers on new materials and medicines every day? This technology doesn't exist yet — but we already have a name for it: the quantum computer. Exponential by nature, it promises a turbo boost for computing power — for the practically unsolvable problems of computer science. The race is on.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Classical encryption must be patched again and again because computers keep growing more powerful. Quantum cryptography is secure by nature: the eavesdropper is always detected.

In the USA, Informatics is mistakenly called "computer science." But the device obscures the view of what really matters: the algorithm, which is what first gives the device its purpose.

A classic data center can be as big as a tennis court. To double the computing power, you need another tennis court. To double the power of a quantum computer, you only need one more logical qubit. Here, exponentiality is built right in.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to superposition, cryptography, and materials simulation.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the coldest place in the world spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers research.
