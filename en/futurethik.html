Futurethics — good innovation in the age of AI

How ethical thought experiments make a good future thinkable.

What should we think about the future? Fear is a poor advisor, and forecasts founder on the complexity of reality. Thought experiments are different. They play with what is thinkable. Only the thinkable can be shaped. Thought experiments turn fear into understanding. That's how spaces for innovation open up. But innovation only asks about feasibility: What can I do? Ethics asks: What should I do? Whoever has many options can choose one that is good — for the family, the company, society. That's Futurethik — good innovation with method: understanding - innovation - ethics.

The thought experiments revolve around questions like why Captain Jean-Luc Picard of the Enterprise doesn't carry a wallet, what Winnetou can teach us about digital leadership, and whether Ötzi would still have been murdered despite a distributed ledger. They ask why there should still be companies and employees, and why aging with dignity is unthinkable without care robots. And they explain why extinction has never been as pleasant for humanity as it is today.

Five Thoughts from the Talk

Thought experiments turn fear into understanding — and understanding into a joy in the future.

Ethics becomes possible the moment we stop thinking there is no alternative.

It is the essence of the future that it is unpredictable — otherwise it would merely be a continuation of the past.

Prosperity endures only if we relentlessly destroy the good with the better.

Agile ethics should not lecture but think along. Not everything that is feasible is good from the outset. But only know-it-alls can know that in advance. Ethics is not a forecast but a daily practice of attentiveness.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: the thought experiments are tailored in advance with the organizer to the industry and audience; the structure stays, the cases change.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: every thought experiment is an invitation to join in — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — and specifically courage: once you've thought a development through to the end, you lose your fear of it. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — the future as a space to shape, not as a threat.
