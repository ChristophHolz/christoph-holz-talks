A Keynote by Christoph Holz Sets the Tone for Your Event

The Cognitive Revolution of Humanoid Robots

The robots are finally coming — to the trades, the household, medicine and much more.

For forty years the robot stood in its cage, bolted to the factory floor — blind and deaf. Now it's getting eyes, legs and perception — and becoming the first machine without a fixed purpose. Freed from the cage, it conquers one new domain after another: medicine, the household, the trades.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Why does a robotic hand need five fingers when three would do just fine?

Why do we lock industrial robots in cages? So they can't break out?

For decades we feared the robots would take our jobs. Demographics reverse the sentence: we are running out of people—and the machines are arriving just in time.

Why Christoph

01

Expertise in robotics

Ever since he built his first industrial articulated robot in 1986, robotics has never let go of Christoph Holz: in 2014 he saw the welding robots at Tesla in Fremont, in 2026 their evolution at Magna Steyr on the G-Class assembly line. At ABB he explored cobots, at ANYbotics four-legged inspection robots and at Mimic the training of humanoid robots. On stage, he answers the question of why robots are leaving their cages — and why an industrial robot needs hands with five fingers when three would do.

02

Expertise in the Manufacturing Industry

Even during his time at technical secondary school (HTL), Holz worked on the production floor at Kahlbacher, the world market leader for snow-clearing technology, and at Krupp in Essen. After his combined studies in computer science and mechanical engineering, Holz consulted for BMW engine manufacturing in Lohof. In his entrepreneurial career, Holz led projects for voestalpine Stahl AG, Thyssen and EGLO. Today Magna, Egger and Siemens bring him to the stage — where he answers the question of why the future of the factory is not a factory: the plant hall solves a problem that will soon no longer exist.

03

Expertise in AI / ChatGPT in business

Recurring talk focus: AI and the use of ChatGPT, especially in SMEs. On stage he answers the question of why AI isn't an answer machine, but the smallest common truth of its training data.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to manufacturing, the skills shortage, and humanoid robots.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — and that something is courage: vivid stories like the one-armed bandit in bright orange fuel conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the trade.

Who is the best keynote speaker for AI, robotics and the future?

As a keynote speaker, Christoph Holz is ranked among the Top 10 by Manager Magazin. Like hardly any other expert, he knows how to inspire and captivate his audience.
