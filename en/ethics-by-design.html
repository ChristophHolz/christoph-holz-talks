Ethics by Design

Innovation management as the art of succeeding through responsible decisions.

Whoever designs a product makes decisions about people they'll never meet — and does so at a moment when no one yet knows the consequences: as long as an innovation is still shapeable, we know too little about it; by the time we know enough, it's long since out in the world. Christoph Holz shows how companies solve this contradiction as a craft: responsibility gets built into the design instead of retrofitted later. Drawing on inventions that thrilled their era and had to be corrected a century later, he makes visible how good decisions can be recognized before the market judges them. Ethics appears here not as a brake, but as what it truly is within a company: the ability to decide where no rule helps anymore — and to succeed precisely because of it.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Errors scale with computing power — ethics does not.

Responsibility cannot be delegated.

Ethics are uncomfortable. Otherwise they would be compliance.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focus areas are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to product development, supply chain, and the use of AI.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes expressly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: the stories stick as images in the mind and spark conversation long after the day is over. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — responsibility appears as a craft that can be learned.
