The Myth of Cyber Fear

What's real about the dangers of the internet — and what's marketing theater

"If hackers can supposedly break in anywhere, anytime, easy as pie, then why is there still money in my account? Why does the economy work when everything could be shut down at any moment? Something doesn't add up. The truth is less spectacular: fear is a business model. Christoph Holz separates real danger from marketing theater — from total surveillance, which technically doesn't even work, to the mundane risk that actually counts: the human, the weak password, the update that was never installed. A calm, optimistic look at cybersecurity. Because whoever separates theater from danger acts effectively instead of in a panic. Whoever understands how, wins."

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Anytime, anywhere, evil hackers can break in—supposedly. So why does anything still work at all, if everything is so vulnerable? Something doesn't add up here.

Evil hackers can break in anywhere at any time — supposedly. Just how dangerous it really is, anyone can check for themselves on their own bank account. Why is there still money available there? It should have been stolen long ago. Why does anything work at all if everything is so vulnerable?

Digital mass surveillance is an unsolvable problem of computer science. At best, you can catch stupid terrorists with it. That only raises the IQ of the remaining population. Even though the relevant US programs have failed, our belief in surveillance helps us behave. The legend of mass surveillance is a disciplinary measure.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to cybersecurity, risk literacy, and AI.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the radar station that works even when switched off spark conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens your team.
