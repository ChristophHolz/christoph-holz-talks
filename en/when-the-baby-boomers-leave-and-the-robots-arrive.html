A Keynote by Christoph Holz Sets the Tone for Your Event

When the baby boomers leave and the robots arrive

How to steer your company through the twin upheaval of demographics and artificial intelligence

For decades we feared: „The robots are taking our jobs.“ Demographics flip the sentence around. We're running out of people — and the machines are arriving just in time. When the baby boomers retire, they take decades of quiet knowledge with them; when AI comes to work, it first takes over the bureaucracy no one misses anyway. In five thought experiments, Christoph Holz shows how entire industries profit from the upheaval driven by demographics and artificial intelligence — and why the association is the only one that can orchestrate this transition for all its members at once. Those who understand how, win.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

When the boomer leaves, he takes forty years of silent knowledge with him. The most valuable thing in any company is in no manual — only AI can save it.

It's not about replacing people, but about filling positions for which there are no people left.

For decades we feared the robots would take our jobs. Demographics reverse the sentence: we are running out of people—and the machines are arriving just in time.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to skills shortages, knowledge retention and robots.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the tacit knowledge of the baby boomers spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers the industry.

Christoph Holz's Expertise

Industrial robots

Ever since he built his first industrial articulated-arm robot in 1986, industrial robotics has never let go of Christoph Holz: in 2014 he saw the welding robots at Tesla in Fremont, and in 2026 their next generation at Magna Steyr on the G-Class assembly line. At ABB he explored cobots. On stage, he answers the question of why robots are leaving their cages — and why an industrial robot needs hands with five fingers when three would do.

Autonomous Mobility

Autonomous mobility follows Christoph Holz from the road all the way into the air: in 2014 he saw the welding robots at Tesla in Fremont, in 2026 the production of the G-Class at Magna Steyr. In Silicon Valley he found the autonomous Nuro delivery van parked at the curb and experienced the shock of his first autonomous cab ride in San Francisco; at ARX Robotics in Munich, the autonomous ground systems for Europe's security. On stage he answers the question of why the car is becoming an app — and the driver is moving to the home office.

TV expert

Christoph Holz appears regularly as a TV expert on digitalization and AI — including on RTL, ServusTV, ProSieben, Sat.1, n-tv, WELT, Deutsche Welle, Hamburg1, ORF and CNBC.

Lecturer in Digital Ethics

As a lecturer, Holz teaches Digital Ethics to aspiring engineers at the University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria. Ethics must not moralize with a wagging finger. As Ethics by Design, it has to be part of the development process from the very start.
