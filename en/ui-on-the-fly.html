UI on the Fly

When the individual user interface only comes into being the moment you open it.

Software was always a building. Fixed rooms, fixed paths, fixed buttons — and people learned the floor plan. Generative AI turns that around: the interface only comes into being the moment you open it. Built for this person, this task, this moment. Then it's gone again. Operation becomes an answer rather than an edifice. At last the software adapts to people, not people to the software.

The thought experiments ask what remains of a brand when no one sees its design anymore, whom you train when tomorrow the interface no longer exists, and who is liable for a button no human designed. And they explain why an eighty-year-old and a developer will use the same software in the future — yet never see the same screen.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Marketing used to mean staying in people's minds. From now on, it means staying in the machine's model.

Thank God the AIs hallucinate. Because it forces us to still take responsibility. Responsibility, of course, is an imposition.

Industrialization means shaping people into consumers so they fit the products. Artificial intelligence reverses this process: everyone gets what fits them.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Is the talk technical or for decision-makers?

For decision-makers: it's about investments, responsibility and brand. Technology gets explained, not assumed.

Is our industry addressed?

Yes: the examples are tailored to the industry and audience in advance with the organizer; the structure stays, the cases change.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: every thought experiment is an invitation to join in — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Courage: whoever has thought the development through to the end can shape it. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic.
