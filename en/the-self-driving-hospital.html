The self-driving hospital

Thought experiments on the future of medical work

Did I become a doctor to compete with software over the better diagnosis — or to accompany my patients on their road to recovery? Did I become a surgeon to spend countless hours in inhuman postures cutting into people — or to heal them? Is it too much to ask of AI that it take the routine off medicine's shoulders and give back what made it a calling?

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Whoever fears that software diagnoses better has forgotten that no one gets well from a diagnosis, but from a hand that holds them.

The robot detects the fall before it happens — only humans still detect the tears.

A surgeon should heal, not spend hours hunched in an inhuman posture. Let the robot hold the blade, and the doctor can lead the treatment again.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to AI diagnostics, surgical robotics, and predictive health.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the self-driving hospital spark conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the doctors.

AI in Healthcare

Christoph Holz knows care from both sides: during his community service with the Red Cross in St. Johann he pulled people from crashed cars and brought elderly people back to their homes after Christmas. His wife Carmen is an anesthesiologist, palliative care physician and ethics consultant — his daily sparring partner for the borderline cases of medicine. For the Helios hospitals, holzweg built the KDOK data capture system, for Novartis the microsite system and the Nicotinell app. On stage, he answers the question of what happens when the care robot rings twice — and where AI helps in care while humans remain irreplaceable.

Emotional AI

Christoph Holz checked at the source whether machines can read feelings: at Tawny.AI in Munich he saw how AI recognizes emotions from faces, at audEERING how it reads moods from the voice, at jambit the applications across industries — plus the SHORE real-time facial recognition from Fraunhofer IIS. On stage he answers the question of why AI can recognize emotions without ever feeling — and what that means for care, consulting or advertising.
