The Human Coder in the Age of AI

The future isn't programmed — it's trained.

Did I become a programmer to type code — or to solve problems? If coding gets democratized, who needs developers? Does off-the-shelf software have a future? Why is software engineering actually not engineering at all? Christoph Holz walks the audience through five thought experiments that remap software development, profession, and industry.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

The future is no longer shaped, it's coded. We nerds weren't prepared for that responsibility. We just wanted to play.

Human strength isn't calculating faster than the machine — it's knowing what's worth calculating.

Information technology is not technology. Technology is the supplement to physical labor. IT is the supplement to mental effort.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and key topics are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to AI in software engineering, prototyping, and new business models.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the Lady Lovelace objection of 1843 spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers developers.
