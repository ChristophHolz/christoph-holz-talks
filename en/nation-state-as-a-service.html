Nation state as a service

What does Facebook still lack to be a real state? Estonia already has it.

On the journey through cyberspace, your Google ID matters far more than your passport. Facebook has a police force. Computer games have a currency. We usually only know all that from nation-states. Nigeria's central bank has a cryptocurrency. Estonia has a fully automated, impartial, citizen-friendly online bureaucracy. Australia is listed in a corporate register. That things won't stay the way they are is obvious. To figure out where we want to go, we first need to know what's actually going on.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

Fully automated bureaucracy is not only incorruptible and impartial, but so swift that Europe's economic growth can finally catch up with global reality.

Politics is the futile art of overcoming one's own irrelevance while patronizing people who know better themselves.

It is the supreme feat of political manipulation to steer our surveillance skepticism toward corporations—and thereby away from the state, whose true instrument of power it is.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to your industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to e-government, decentralization and digital identity.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — and that something is courage: vivid stories like Estonia's fully automated online bureaucracy fuel conversations that last well beyond the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that strengthens the region.
