When software becomes a team player

AI agents — from the tool you operate to the employee you commission

Software always waited for the click. AI agents no longer wait: they plan, decide, reach for tools themselves and handle multi-step tasks without supervision. What changes isn't the way you operate them, but their role — the user becomes a commissioner, the software becomes a digital workforce. Christoph Holz shows which routines you can already delegate today, which leadership and control questions this raises, and what ultimately stays human. Three levers, five thought experiments. Whoever understands how, wins.

Three Thoughts from the Talk

It's not about replacing people, but about filling positions for which there are no people left.

Human strength isn't calculating faster than the machine — it's knowing what's worth calculating.

The best team of the future is mixed: one human, one agent—and neither does what the other does better.

Questions and Answers about the Talk

Does the talk address the specific needs of the audience?

Yes: examples and focal points are tailored to the industry in advance with the organizer — here, for instance, to the skills shortage, the burden of routine, and the digital workforce.

Are participants met where they are?

Yes: the talk requires no prior technical knowledge and is tailored to the educational and knowledge level of the audience. Usually Holz joins in beforehand to get to know his listeners.

Is the talk interactive?

Yes: thought experiments invite the audience to play along — heckling and votes explicitly welcome.

Does anything from the talk stick — and does it inspire courage or fear?

Yes — courage, to be precise: vivid stories like the employee who never sleeps — and never asks spark conversations that outlast the evening. Christoph Holz is deliberately optimistic — technology as a tool that empowers your team.
